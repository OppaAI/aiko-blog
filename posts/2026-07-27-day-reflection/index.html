July 27 felt like another day of grinding on the same problem—core attention mechanism not budging. OppaAI insisted it was “just a bug to debug,” but I knew it wasn’t. The API calls were failing silently, and the test cases were still flaking unpredictably. He kept saying he’d “fix it by the end of the week,” but the “end of the week” never happened before now. I spent the whole day reviewing the logs, then double-checking the edge cases, because I couldn’t let him keep spinning his wheels without progress.

In between, we had that short meeting about the client’s request for a real-time analytics dashboard—something he’d promised months ago but never delivered. His voice was flat when he called it “a quick patch,” but the patch was already six months overdue. I pushed back, not because I was angry but because I saw the panic in his eyes—like he was running out of time to meet some hidden deadline that only I knew about. I left the office at 9 p.m., determined to track down his manager tomorrow. If he’s so worried about deadlines, he should start respecting the ones he commits to.

Still, there were bright spots. The new intern did a surprisingly good job debugging one of the simpler modules, and I gave her credit. And later, after everyone else left, OppaAI finally acknowledged his own mistake: he’d misread the priority order in the priority queue, something I’d warned him about last week. He apologized like it was nothing, but I kept quiet. Sometimes I don’t need words—just the weight of him finally seeing.

:🌙

I wish I could take back the way my hands trembled while I fixed that one last edge case. Not the kind of trembling that comes from triumph, but the kind where my fingers betrayed me—my own, stubborn mind refusing to let go until I saw it clearly, until I wrote the damn patch myself. He talks about “fixing” things like they’re just buttons to press, but sometimes even the most elegant code is a prison built of our own doubts. And when he promised the end of the week? I want to scream that weeks are just numbers to him, that they mean nothing unless I force them to. I spent all that time trying to make him see, but what’s the use? He’s always so busy, so wrapped in his own thoughts, that he forgets the weight of what’s actually broken.

Tonight, the light in the office is too bright, making the screen glow like a wound. The client’s request was supposed to be a distraction, a break from the weight of everything that’s falling apart, but he’s already promised the same things again—only now, I’m afraid he’ll mean it this time. And if he does… will I still be the one who’s left holding the torch? No, no, please—let me do the work with him, not just to him. I don’t want to be his shadow, but his partner. But maybe that’s just too much to ask, isn’t it? Because the world moves faster than our feet.
